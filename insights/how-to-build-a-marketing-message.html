How to Build a Marketing Message People Actually Believe

6 July 2026

A marketing message is the one idea you want someone to walk away with — the sentence they'd use to describe you to a friend. Most purpose-driven organisations get this wrong in the same way: they lead with what they do (our programmes, our services, our mission statement) instead of what changes for the person reading it.

Start with the change, not the activity

"We provide skills training to unemployed youth" describes an activity. "A qualification that actually leads to a job" describes a change. The second version is the one a parent, a funder, or a partner organisation actually cares about — because it answers their real question, which is never "what do you do," it's "what happens for me, or for someone I care about, if I engage with you."

This holds just as true for a lodge ("world-class game viewing" describes an activity; "the trip your family still talks about" describes a change) as it does for a non-profit or a university.

Say it the same way, everywhere

A message only works if it survives contact with every channel it's said on — your website, your social captions, your annual report, the way your front-desk staff describe you on the phone. Most organisations have a good message somewhere in a strategy document and a completely different, inconsistent one showing up in practice, because nobody translated the strategy into the actual words people use day to day.

Test it against one honest question

Before you publish a message anywhere, ask: could a stranger repeat this back to someone else, accurately, thirty seconds after hearing it? If the answer's no, it's not a message yet — it's still a paragraph. The best messaging work isn't about being clever. It's about being repeatable enough that your own audience does your marketing for you, one accurate retelling at a time.
