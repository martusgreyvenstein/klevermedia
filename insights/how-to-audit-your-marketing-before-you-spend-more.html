How to Audit Your Own Marketing Before You Spend More

15 September 2026

Most organisations decide to spend more on marketing before they've checked whether what they already have is working. An hour spent auditing what's actually happening right now is usually worth more than the next campaign brief.

Start with where people actually land

Pull up your website and your last month of social posts, and look at them the way a stranger would: someone who's never heard of you, arriving with a real question. Does the homepage answer what they came to ask in the first ten seconds, or does it lead with your mission statement? Do the social posts sound like a specific organisation, or could the logo be swapped out for a competitor's without changing a word?

Write down every place this feels generic. That list is your audit, and it's usually more honest than anything a formal report would tell you.

Check whether your channels agree with each other

Open your website, your most recent newsletter, and your social bio side by side. If they describe what you do in three different ways, that's not a content problem, it's a message that never got locked down. No amount of new content fixes an inconsistent message; it just spreads the inconsistency further.

Look at what you can actually measure

If you can't say which channel brought you your last three enquiries, donations, or applications, that's the real finding. Not "we need more content," but "we can't tell what's working," which is a measurement gap, not a content gap. Fix that before spending on anything else, or you'll be back here in six months with the same question.

This audit is the first thing we do in every marketing strategy engagement, and it's often what tells us whether the real gap is in planning, in social media management, or somewhere else entirely.
